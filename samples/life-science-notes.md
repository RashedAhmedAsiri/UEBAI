# Chapter 4: Vertebrates

## Mammals

A mammal is an animal with fur that nurses its young with milk. Examples include dogs, bats, dolphins and people.

There are three groups of mammals: monotremes, which lay eggs; marsupials, which carry their young in a pouch; and placental mammals, whose young grow inside the mother attached to a placenta.

Bats are the only mammals capable of true flight. Whales and dolphins are mammals that live entirely in water but still breathe air with lungs.

Mammals keep a constant body temperature. Fur, fat and sweat glands help them stay warm or cool down.

## Reptiles

Reptiles are cold-blooded vertebrates with dry, scaly skin. Snakes, lizards, turtles and crocodiles are reptiles.

Most reptiles lay leathery eggs on land. Because they are ectotherms, reptiles bask in the sun to warm their bodies.

# Chapter 5: Energy in Living Things

## Photosynthesis

Plants make their own food through photosynthesis. Using sunlight, they combine carbon dioxide from the air with water from the soil to make glucose, and they release oxygen.

Photosynthesis happens in chloroplasts. The green pigment chlorophyll captures the energy of sunlight.

Almost all food chains begin with photosynthesis, and the oxygen we breathe comes from it.

## Cellular Respiration

Cellular respiration is how cells release the energy stored in glucose. It happens in the mitochondria and needs oxygen.

The equation is roughly the reverse of photosynthesis: glucose + oxygen produces carbon dioxide + water + energy (ATP).

When oxygen is scarce, some cells use fermentation instead, which releases much less energy.
